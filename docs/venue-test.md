# Venue comparison test: how to run it

Purpose: honest, informal numbers for the pitch and Devpost ("n = 5, informal: 6.2 of 8 with
the palace against 4.0 of 8 with a plain list"). It is not a study and we never call it one.

## Setup (once)

1. Open the deployed site, or a local production build, with `?compare` on the demo laptop.
   It works offline.
2. Results stay in this browser only. Use one laptop and one browser for everyone, or copy
   the CSV from each device at the end.

## For each volunteer (about 4 to 4.5 minutes)

1. Recruit people who have **not** seen Judge Mode: it uses the same eight carpal bones, so
   anyone who has tried it already knows half the answers. Skip pre-med and biochemistry
   students if you can; they may know one of the lists already.
2. **Alternate the order by hand** so it comes out balanced with only a few people:
   odd volunteers open `?compare=list-first`, even volunteers `?compare=palace-first`.
   (Plain `?compare` picks at random, which can put everyone in the same order.)
3. Say only this: "You will memorise two short lists in two different ways, and be tested on
   each. It takes about four minutes. Just follow the screen." Do not explain the method.
4. Do not help, hint or react while they work. Stay quiet during both study phases.
5. When the results screen shows, let them look, then press **Next participant**.

## What each volunteer does

- **List arm:** eight intermediates of the citric acid cycle as a numbered list for 46 seconds.
- **Palace arm:** the eight carpal bones as a timed walk, also 46 seconds including camera travel.
- After each: 20 seconds of typed sums (not scored), then eight ordered multiple-choice questions.

## Afterwards

1. Press **Copy as CSV** and paste it into the team doc.
2. Fill in the pitch line and the Devpost "(to fill in)" slots with the two means and n.
3. Report it exactly as it is, including if the palace did not win.

## Limits to state if asked

- Different lists in each arm (the list arm's meanings are longer); same-session recall only.
- About 18 of the palace arm's 46 seconds are camera travel, so it has less reading time.
- Eight-option multiple choice allows guessing (1 in 8) and elimination, in both arms.
- Small n; order balanced by hand, not randomised.
- If the carpal deck was edited on this device ("Strengthen this image"), the palace arm uses
  the edited images. Use a fresh browser profile for the test.
